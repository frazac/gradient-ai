    Gradient AI

    Levels of AI use, based on the AIAS

    Teaching profile · humanities

    Five levels to say, before a test or task, how much AI can be part of it, and how: essays, translations, comments on texts, research. A profile for literature, philosophy, history, languages and social sciences.

    This page is in Simple English. There is already a better English text: the original AI Assessment Scale (AIAS) by Mike Perkins, Leon Furze, Jasper Roe and Jason MacVaugh. Read it first if English is easy for you.

    Gradient AI started in Italian. We wrote this English version for people who are still learning English. We follow the plain language standard ISO 24495-1:2023 and the rules of the Simple English Wikipedia: common words, short sentences, one idea in each sentence.

    Based on the AI Assessment Scale (AIAS) v2 by Mike Perkins, Leon Furze, Jasper Roe and Jason MacVaugh · CC BY-NC-SA 4.0

    Level 1 · No AI

    On your own

    For teachers

    Students do the task with no AI, in a room where you can see the work. They use only their own reading, their own understanding and their own writing.

    Examples of tasks

    • A translation done in class.
    • A comment on a text, written in class. An oral exam.
    • Work on an old document with no devices.

    Gradient AI · Level 1 · On your own

    [Gradient AI · Level 1 · On your own](https://frazac.github.io/gradient-ai/en/humanities/#livello-1)

    For students

    Do not use AI at any step. This task shows how you read, understand and write on your own.

    Level 2 · AI only before you start

    Ideas

    For teachers

    AI can help only before the real work starts: to find ideas, to plan an essay, to start research. You assess how students develop and support these ideas on their own.

    Examples of tasks

    • Ideas for the main point of an essay.
    • A plan for an essay.
    • A first list of books to read. Check every title.

    Gradient AI · Level 2 · Ideas

    [Gradient AI · Level 2 · Ideas](https://frazac.github.io/gradient-ai/en/humanities/#livello-2)

    For students

    You can use AI to find ideas, to plan and to start your research. You write the final text yourself. Check every book or source it names.

    Level 3 · AI helps, you check everything

    Working together

    For teachers

    AI can help to do the task: it can suggest words, write a first draft or comment on an argument. Students check and rewrite what the AI gives. This shows that they understand it.

    Examples of tasks

    • A first draft, then changed and with notes.
    • Your own translation next to an AI translation, with a talk about the differences.
    • Comments from AI on the argument of an essay.

    Gradient AI · Level 3 · Working together

    [Gradient AI · Level 3 · Working together](https://frazac.github.io/gradient-ai/en/humanities/#livello-3)

    For students

    You can use AI for some parts of the task, like a first draft or comments. Check and rewrite everything it makes. Say how you used AI.

    Level 4 · People lead the AI

    Directing

    For teachers

    AI can be used for any part of the task, or the task needs AI. Students lead the AI to the goal. You assess their critical thinking, how they check sources, and how they explain texts.

    Examples of tasks

    • Building a large set of texts and studying it with AI.
    • A digital edition of a text.
    • A file with the instructions given to AI, the source checks and the choices made.

    Gradient AI · Level 4 · Directing

    [Gradient AI · Level 4 · Directing](https://frazac.github.io/gradient-ai/en/humanities/#livello-4)

    For students

    You can use AI a lot, as you like or as the task says. What matters is how you lead it and how well you think about what it gives you.

    Level 5 · AI as a place to try new things

    Exploring

    For teachers

    Students use AI in creative ways to ask new questions about texts and sources. Teachers and students plan the task together, to try new uses of AI in the subject.

    Examples of tasks

    • Methods from the digital humanities.
    • Studying the errors and limits of AI models on old texts.
    • New ways to read and publish texts.

    Gradient AI · Level 5 · Exploring

    [Gradient AI · Level 5 · Exploring](https://frazac.github.io/gradient-ai/en/humanities/#livello-5)

    For students

    Use AI in creative ways to do the task. You can plan new methods together with your teacher.

    How to use the badges

    There are four ways, from the easiest to the most flexible. The examples use level 3 and link to its part of this page. Shape, colours and weight follow your choices in the panel above.

    1. Text only, with a link

    For documents, emails, learning platforms and websites: one line that gives the level and links to it. The button “Copy text with link” under each level copies this line for you. In Word, Google Docs or an email, the link still works when you paste it.

    In HTML:

    2. PNG image

    For slides, documents and platforms that only take images. Each stamp comes at 128 and 512 px (icon: 64 and 256; label: 48 and 192 px high), as an outline or filled.

    All PNG files

    3. SVG file

    A vector file: it stays sharp at any size, and you can change it in Illustrator, Inkscape or Figma. The text uses a system font.

    All SVG files

    4. JavaScript

    It works like Lucide: you mark the place with an attribute, and the library draws the badge, with the colours and weight you want.

    Options: variant (stamp, icon, label) · lang (en or it) · color · from/to (a gradient in five steps) · palette (five colours) · weight (1–3) · filled · size (px) · link (by default the badge links to its level on this site; false removes the link, or give the address of a profile). To get only the SVG text: GradientAI.toSvg(3, {variant: 'stamp', lang: 'en'}).

    Notes on use

    • The levels do not say how good a task is. They only say how much AI is part of it, and how.
    • You can only be sure of level 1 in a room where you can see the work. For work done at home, expect that students may use AI in any way. Plan the task with this in mind.
    • Choose the level from what students need to learn. Tell students the level before they start. Also tell them what they must report, for example the instructions they gave to the AI.
