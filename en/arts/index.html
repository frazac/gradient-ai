    Gradient AI

    Levels of AI use, based on the AIAS

    Introduction

    Five gradients to show how much AI models are part of a piece of work, a project, a task or a simple text. On this site you can also change these badges. You can choose a context, for example teaching, with a different version for each kind of study. You can also change how they look: their colours, weight and size.

    This page is in Simple English. There is already a better English text: the original AI Assessment Scale (AIAS) by Mike Perkins, Leon Furze, Jasper Roe and Jason MacVaugh. Read it first if English is easy for you.

    Gradient AI started in Italian. We wrote this English version for people who are still learning English. We follow the plain language standard ISO 24495-1:2023 and the rules of the Simple English Wikipedia: common words, short sentences, one idea in each sentence.

    Gradient 1On your own

    On your own (no AI)

    Students do the task with no AI, in a room where you can see the work. They use only their own hands, their own ears and their own technique.

    Examples of tasks

    • Drawing from life in class. A quick sketch by hand.
    • Playing an instrument in an exam. Improvising.
    • Music theory exercises on paper.

    Gradient AI · Level 1 · On your own (no AI)

    [Gradient AI · Level 1 · On your own (no AI)](https://getgradient.it/en/arts/#livello-1)

    For students (Profile: Arts teaching, change)

    Do not use AI at any step. This task shows what you can make with your own skills.

    Gradient 2Ideas

    Ideas

    AI can help only before the real work starts: to collect images and ideas, to try different versions of a concept. You assess how students develop these ideas with their own skills.

    Examples of tasks

    • A mood board of images and ideas.
    • Versions of a concept, to talk about in class.
    • First research on images or music.

    Gradient AI · Level 2 · Ideas

    [Gradient AI · Level 2 · Ideas](https://getgradient.it/en/arts/#livello-2)

    For students (Profile: Arts teaching, change)

    You can use AI to collect images and ideas and to try versions of a concept. You make the drawings, the music or the model yourself.

    Gradient 3Working together

    Working together

    AI can help to make the work: it can make sketches, suggest music or give comments. Students choose, change and rework what the AI gives, and explain their choices.

    Examples of tasks

    • Sketches made by AI, then drawn again by hand.
    • Music arranged with AI tools, then checked by listening.
    • A text about the project, written with AI, then rewritten.

    Gradient AI · Level 3 · Working together

    [Gradient AI · Level 3 · Working together](https://getgradient.it/en/arts/#livello-3)

    For students (Profile: Arts teaching, change)

    You can use AI for some parts of the task. Choose and change what it makes with your own hands. Say how you used AI.

    Gradient 4Directing

    Directing

    AI can be used for any part of the task, or the task needs AI. Students are the artistic directors. You assess their vision, how their choices fit together and how much control they keep over the result.

    Examples of tasks

    • A media project where AI makes material under the student's direction.
    • A portfolio that shows the process and explains each choice.
    • A show or exhibition planned with AI tools.

    Gradient AI · Level 4 · Directing

    [Gradient AI · Level 4 · Directing](https://getgradient.it/en/arts/#livello-4)

    For students (Profile: Arts teaching, change)

    You can use AI a lot, as you like or as the task says. What matters is your artistic direction. Explain every choice.

    Gradient 5Exploring

    Exploring

    AI is the material, the tool or the subject of the artwork. Teachers and students plan the task together, to try new languages and new ways of making.

    Examples of tasks

    • Artworks that use AI as their material or as their subject.
    • Performances and installations.
    • Tools for making art, built for the project.

    Gradient AI · Level 5 · Exploring

    [Gradient AI · Level 5 · Exploring](https://getgradient.it/en/arts/#livello-5)

    For students (Profile: Arts teaching, change)

    Use AI in creative ways, also as the material of your work. You can plan new methods together with your teacher.

    How to use the badges

    There are four ways, from the easiest to the most flexible. The examples use level 3 and link to its part of this page. Shape, colours and weight follow your choices in the panel above.

    A fixed copy, or always up to date?

    • Downloaded files (SVG, PNG): they are your own copy. They do not change.
    • Codes on this page (PNG image, SVG file, JavaScript): they are linked to the version in the address. Today this is version 0.7.1. You keep seeing this version when the project changes, until you change the number.
    • This website always shows the latest version. The text with a link brings people here, to the latest version of the level.

    If you need to state a level in a way that does not change, for example in a contract, a task or a call for projects, use a downloaded file or one of the codes below.

    1. Text only, with a link

    For documents, emails, learning platforms and websites: one line that gives the level and links to it. The button “Copy text with link” under each level copies this line for you. In Word, Google Docs or an email, the link still works when you paste it.

    In HTML:

    2. PNG image

    For slides, documents and platforms that only take images. Each stamp comes at 128 and 512 px (pictogram: 64 and 256; label: 48 and 192 px high), as an outline or filled.

    All PNG files

    3. SVG file

    A vector file: it stays sharp at any size, and you can change it in Illustrator, Inkscape or Figma. The text uses a system font.

    All SVG files

    4. JavaScript

    You mark the place with an attribute, and the library draws the badge, with the colours and weight you want.

    Options: variant (stamp, icon, label) · lang (en or it) · color · from/to (a gradient in five steps) · palette (five colours) · weight (1–3) · filled · size (px) · link (by default the badge links to its level on this site; false removes the link, or give the address of a profile). To get only the SVG text: GradientAI.toSvg(3, {variant: 'stamp', lang: 'en'}).

    Notes on use

    • The levels do not say how good a task is. They only say how much AI is part of it, and how.
    • You can only be sure of level 1 in a room where you can see the work. For work done at home, expect that students may use AI in any way. Plan the task with this in mind.
    • Choose the level from what students need to learn. Tell students the level before they start. Also tell them what they must report, for example the instructions they gave to the AI.
    • To state a level in a way that does not change, see A fixed copy, or always up to date?

    How this project used AI

    Content

    The author made the adaptation and the main choices: the names of the levels, the profiles, and the focus on work and on teaching. The texts were written together with AI (Claude), following the author's directions. The author reads, corrects and checks them against the AIAS v2.

    Colours

    AI suggested a first set of ten colours and made a tool to adjust them. It also measured the contrast and the difference between the colours. The author chose the direction and adjusted the colours by eye. AI chose some of the final colours, following the author's directions.

    Website, code and design

    The website, the JavaScript library, the build tools, the badges and the GitHub repository were made with Claude Code. The author set the goals and the design choices, and checked and tested the result.

    Host a research project

    Gradient AI can be studied in real use. For example: how teachers, students and clients read the levels, if the levels help people say how they used AI, and where the line between two levels is not clear. Do you work at a university, a department or a research centre? Do you want to host research on this? The author is open to working with you: a research grant, a visiting fellowship or a joint research project.
